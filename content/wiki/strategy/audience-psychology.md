# Audience Psychology

## Core lesson

Good content creation is really applied psychology. If you can get a viewer to stop scrolling, watch to the end, and take action, you're doing human psychology, whether you've studied it or not. You don't need a psychology degree, but you do need to clearly understand your target audience.

> "Anyone who is good at creating content is secretly good at human psychology."

> "If you can get somebody to stop on your video, watch to the end, comment, or do whatever ManyChat that you want them to do, in some way, shape, or form, my friend, you are good at human psychology."

> "What it does mean is that you need to clearly understand what your audience likes, doesn't like, is interested in, stands for, stands against, and create content around that criteria."

Creator mentions this framework is what clicked for clients in a "two hour long masterclass."

## Framework: Audience Psychology Profile

Analyze your target audience across five dimensions before creating content:

1. What they **like**
2. What they **don't like**
3. What they're **interested in**
4. What they **stand for**
5. What they **stand against**

Build content around these five criteria so it connects psychologically instead of just being generic.

## Framework: The Core Engagement Objective

Every piece of high-performing content has to do three things, in order:

1. Get the viewer to **stop** on the video (the hook).
2. **Retain** them to watch to the end (pacing).
3. Spur them to **take action** (comment, DM/ManyChat, follow, etc.) — the CTA.

## How to apply

- Write out your audience's profile: what they like, dislike, are interested in, stand for, stand against.
- Plan topics and messaging that align with or directly speak to those five points.
- Structure every post as: strong hook to stop the scroll → paced narrative/visuals to hold attention → clear call to action at the end.
